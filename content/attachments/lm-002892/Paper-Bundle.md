## Main distinction

$$
\boxed{
D_t^{\rm resolve}
\neq
D_t^{\rm frontier}.
}
$$

Compiled proof history can theoremically reduce routine-resolution cost under controlled conditions. It does not automatically reduce the cost of discovering the next genuinely new theorem cut.

## Main exact decomposition

$$
\boxed{
\Delta D_t^{\rm resolve}
=
-G_t^{\rm compile}
+
P_t^{\rm drift}.
}
$$

## Main phase metric

$$
\boxed{
\sigma_t
=
\frac{W_t}{D_t+W_t}.
}
$$

## Key status

- Weak DVI: theorem-compatible and empirically testable.
- Strong DVI: open falsifiable hypothesis.
- Verification dominance can coexist with decreasing total cost.
- Frontier hardening is an explicit competing regime.
