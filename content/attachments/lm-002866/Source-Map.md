# Source Map — Paper 07

## External academic anchors

- Fong, Malhotra & Margalit (2019): public memories of past politicians are actively used in contemporary political debate and can alter the persuasiveness of arguments in policy domains associated with those politicians.
- Winger & Oliver (2024): former presidents can retain rhetorical and elite-cue influence after leaving formal office, showing post-office influence without governing authority.
- Cullinane & Ellis (2018): presidential legacy is constructed through achievements, failures, commemoration, media, monuments and later generations rather than being fixed at departure.
- Zubrzycki & Woźny (2020): collective memory contributes to national identity, political legitimation, reckoning with difficult pasts and transnational memory politics.
- Gugushvili, Kabachnik & Kirvalidze (2017): heroes and villains can be rehabilitated, vilified or contested; national identity shapes reputational politics.
- Cirone & Pepinsky (2022): historical effects can persist over decade-plus timescales and influence later political and social outcomes.
- Merton (1968): the Matthew effect provides a general model of cumulative recognition.
