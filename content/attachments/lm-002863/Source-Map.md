# Source Map — Paper 04

## External academic anchors

- Endogenous State Capacity: state capacity has multiple dimensions and is shaped by elite conflict and political incentives.
- Ways out of Authoritarianism (Oxford Handbook, 2025): authoritarian exits can be regular or irregular; regular transitions more often support democratization, while violent/irregular collapse can lead to new autocracy or state failure.
- Elite Change without Regime Change: crises can induce elite-coalition reconfiguration that preserves authoritarian rule rather than ending it.
- Autocratic Breakdown and Regime Transitions: autocratic leader exit can produce democracy, regime persistence, or a new autocracy.
- Regime Change Cascades: focal points, lame-duck leadership, elite coordination and structural preconditions affect cascades; cascades do not necessarily yield democracy.
- Critical Junctures literature: institutional change is concentrated in bounded periods with heightened contingency and coalition-building.
- Preconditions for Pacted Transitions: major political crises can open negotiated transitions when elites-in-power and opposition have bargaining counterparts.
- Authoritarian-Led Democratization: low transition risk and incumbent organizational strength can make top-down transition strategically rational.
